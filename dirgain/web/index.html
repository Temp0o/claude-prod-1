DirGain

Per-direction mouse gain, measured on you, applied to any mouse.

Simulation

Simulation mode: a synthetic player stands in for your mouse, so you can see the whole flow. Remove ?sim=1 from the address to calibrate yourself.

Step 1

Match your game

You will flick to targets with the same hand movement your game uses, so the bias DirGain measures is the one you actually aim with.

Task style

Advanced

Use Chrome or Edge on Windows: they deliver raw mouse counts, so Windows pointer settings do not matter here.

Step 2

Calibrate

Flick to each target and click it. A block is 72 targets in 24 directions at three distances and takes about two minutes. Your current profile stays on while you play, so every round measures what is left.

Step 3

Prove it helps

A blind test: blocks with and without your profile's directional shape, in an order you are not told. Both keep the same average speed, so only the direction effect is tested. Keep the profile only if it makes you faster.

Step 4

Use it in your games

Two ways to apply your profile on Windows. Both work in games because they change the mouse signal before any game sees it.

Step 5

Your data

Everything stays in this browser. Download the raw data to keep it or move it to another computer.
